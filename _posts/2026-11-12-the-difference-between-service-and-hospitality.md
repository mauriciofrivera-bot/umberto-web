### Great service meets expectations. True hospitality creates memories.

A lot of people think service and hospitality are the same thing.



They’re not.

#### **Service is technical. Hospitality is emotional.**



Service is bringing a plate correctly to the table.

 Hospitality is understanding the right moment to do it.



Service can be taught relatively quickly.

 Hospitality requires sensitivity.



**You can teach someone how to open a bottle of wine.**

 **Much harder is teaching someone how to read a table.**



Understanding who wants conversation.

 Who wants privacy.

 Who is celebrating.

 Who had a terrible day.



And often, the smallest details completely change a dinner experience.



The tone of your voice.

 The right pacing.

 A well-timed smile.

 Or simply knowing when NOT to interrupt.



**The best restaurant experiences I’ve ever seen were not necessarily technically perfect.**



**They felt human.**



Because at the end of the day, people are not only looking for food and wine.



They’re looking for attention.

 Connection.

 Emotion.



And that’s what transforms a restaurant into a place people truly want to return to.



You can have incredible dishes, rare wines, and a beautiful dining room.



**But if the human side is missing, people feel it immediately.**



**Real hospitality is not performance.**

 **It’s not acting.**



It’s making someone feel important without making it feel forced.



And honestly, that’s probably one of the hardest things to do well.
