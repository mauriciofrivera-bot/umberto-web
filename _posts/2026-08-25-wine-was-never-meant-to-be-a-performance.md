Wine used to be conversation.

Now it often feels like content.

Bottles chosen for photos.
Restaurants chosen for stories.
Wine glasses held more for Instagram than for actually drinking.

And the problem isn’t social media itself.

### The problem starts when aesthetics completely replace experience.

Today, many people drink what “looks right” to drink.

Champagne because it looks luxurious.
Orange wine because it looks alternative.
Natural wine because it feels trendy.

But very few people stop and ask themselves:

### “Do I actually enjoy this?”

And honestly, that’s where wine begins losing authenticity.

Because wine should feel personal.

### Not performative.

Not like a character people create online.

Some of the best wine moments I remember were never photogenic.

They were long conversations.
Late dinners.
Bottles opened without overthinking them.

Nobody was posting stories.
Nobody was searching for validation.

### People were simply living the moment.

And maybe wine should return to that a little more.
