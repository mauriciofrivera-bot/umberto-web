There’s a sentence I’ve heard for years in the wine world:

“Red wine should be served at room temperature.”

### Yes… but whose room?

Because that rule was created centuries ago in cold European castles, not in modern restaurants running at 78 degrees or during summer heat.

And yet, people still drink overly warm red wine thinking it’s “correct.”

The truth is that many red wines become far better slightly chilled.

Not ice cold.
Not frozen.

Just cool enough to preserve balance, freshness, and drinkability.

When red wine gets too warm:
the alcohol feels aggressive,
the tannins become heavy,
and the wine gets tiring very quickly.

And honestly, sometimes lowering the temperature just a few degrees completely transforms the experience.

Some of the most enjoyable wines I drink during summer are lightly chilled reds:
Frappato,
Gamay,
Pinot Noir,
Etna Rosso,
Grenache.

The problem is that many people are afraid of breaking wine “rules.”

But a lot of those rules are simply habits that turned into dogma.

Wine should create pleasure, not anxiety.

### If a wine tastes better slightly chilled, then serve it that way.

Simple.
